=======================
Who replies to feedback
=======================

In this tutorial you find out which contributors replied to the comments and reviews on their pull requests, look at the pull requests of one person, and put the badge of a person into a README.

You need a project read from GitHub: :doc:`first-project`. It takes about 5 minutes.

The replies of everybody
========================

#. Open the project and choose **Replies** at the top.
#. The table lists everybody who got feedback on a pull request: a comment or review by somebody else, not by a bot. People with an open pull request without a reply come first.
#. Each row has a label: **answered** (green), **only one, not answered** (yellow) or **not answered** (red). **PRs replied to** says how many of their pull requests that count were replied to, such as ``1/3``; **Not replied to and open** and **Not replied to and closed** link those pull requests on GitHub.
#. Above the table, the page says how many contributors it shows of all, with a link to why the others are not shown, such as merged pull requests, which need no reply.

The replies of one person
=========================

#. Choose the label of a person. Their page lists all their pull requests with their titles, those without a reply first, and says why the others need none.
#. Next to the name is their badge: choose it.

The badge in a README
=====================

#. The page of the badge shows the badge and its code for Markdown, reStructuredText, HTML and the plain URL. Choose the tab and press **Copy**.
#. Choose **PNG** instead of **SVG** for places that show no SVG, or open **The badge as JSON** for the numbers behind it.
#. Paste the code into the README of a repository. The badge links back to the replies of the person.
#. Signed in, you can let one of your tokens keep the badge up to date: choose it under **Whose tokens update the badge**, for this badge or for all badges of the project, and press **Allow**.

You know who replies to feedback and have the badge of a person in a README.
